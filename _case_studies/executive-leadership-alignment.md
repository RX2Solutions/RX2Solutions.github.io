## The Situation

The organization's IT structure had grown reactively as the business evolved. While the team had strong individual contributors, there was no consistent leadership framework tying everything together. As a result:

- priorities were unclear
- teams were working in silos
- modernization work stalled
- frustrations were rising across departments
- operational leaders struggled to get reliable execution

The company needed IT leaders who could establish structure, create alignment, provide calm direction, strengthen communication, and guide modernization in a manufacturing environment.

This was not about hiring technologists. It was about securing leaders who could reshape how the function operated.

## What We Did

RX2 approached the engagement with a leadership-first lens, focusing on the behavioral and strategic capabilities needed to transform the department.

### 1. Mapped The Leadership Gaps

We worked directly with executive leadership to understand the current state of IT, where execution was breaking down, the modernization roadmap, team dynamics, cross-functional expectations, and what great leadership would look like in their environment.

### 2. Defined The True Leadership Profile

Instead of simply listing technical skills, we clarified the communication style, change-management experience, operational steadiness, decision-making approach, and credibility needed inside their environment.

### 3. Conducted A Targeted Leadership Search

We targeted candidates who had led modernization efforts, stabilized underperforming teams, created alignment across departments, and built trust in complex operational environments.

### 4. Ensured Organizational Fit

Every candidate was evaluated against the company's culture and pace. We prioritized leaders who brought calm structure, transparent communication, and the ability to balance short-term pressures with long-term strategy.

### 5. Supported Smooth Integration

After placement, RX2 remained involved to ensure clear expectations, executive alignment, visibility into priorities, and the ability to create early wins.

## The Outcome

The two leaders immediately changed the trajectory of the IT function:

- teams gained **clearer direction**
- modernization work regained momentum
- communication across departments improved
- priorities became structured and realistic
- execution discipline increased noticeably

Stakeholders across operations, finance, and production felt the impact. IT shifted from being a bottleneck to becoming a partner that could support growth and operational excellence.

## Strategic Impact

This case demonstrates RX2's ability to deliver leadership that reshapes a function, aligning teams, stabilizing execution, and enabling meaningful progress in complex, operationally driven environments.
